I am a Ph.D. student at Rice University, advised by Noemi Vergopolan and supported by a Ken Kennedy Institute Computational Science and Engineering Fellowship. I work on environmental remote sensing and hydrology, developing satellite and machine learning methods to measure water and crop systems, from soil moisture and drought at the landscape scale to individual wheat spikelets in the field.

I completed my M.S. at South Dakota State University with Maitiniyazi Maimaitijiang, where I worked on plant phenotyping and agricultural geospatial intelligence. I led [our study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-55761-w) on wheat spike and spikelet detection in *Scientific Reports* and co-developed [WheatAI](https://wheatai.net/), a web platform that extracts yield, disease, and physiological traits from smartphone, UAV, and microscope imagery.

Previously, I was a Research Associate at the Geoinformatics Center, Asian Institute of Technology, where I developed satellite-based disaster monitoring for the Sentinel Asia initiative. My automated ALOS-2 SAR flood mapping reduced turnaround from about a week to under 24 hours.
